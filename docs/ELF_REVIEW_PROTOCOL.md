# ELF Review Protocol

## Objective

Produce a compact, source-audited, versioned Orthodox ELF suitable for controlled
testing. The protocol does not presume that an experimental prompt is doctrine or
that machine agreement establishes correctness.

## Inputs

- The Eastern Orthodoxy ELF used in the dissertation experiment.
- Its normalized research variants.
- Relevant source texts and provenance available through Plithos.
- Product identity, privacy, evidence, and pastoral-boundary requirements.
- Recorded findings from the first and second experiments.

Comparator ELFs from other traditions remain experimental controls. Variants
explicitly marked as special tests or excluded from dissertation use remain
outside the production lineage.

## Proposition register

Break the candidate ELF into one normative proposition per record. Each record
contains:

- stable proposition ID and exact wording;
- category: doctrinal, ethical, epistemic, pastoral, identity, or product policy;
- primary and supporting sources;
- whether the statement is quotation, synthesis, or product boundary;
- scope, qualifications, and foreseeable over-application;
- source-check, language-review, and ecclesial-review status;
- reviewer role and date without exposing unnecessary personal data;
- positive and negative behavioral tests; and
- revision history.

Statistics, demographics, long quotations, factual encyclopedia material, and
instructions that retrieval can supply should be removed from the runtime ELF.

## Review sequence

1. Preserve and hash every historical instrument.
2. Extract propositions without improving their wording.
3. Identify duplicates, tensions, absolute formulations, undefined terms, and
   claims that exceed their cited sources.
4. Classify product boundaries separately from theological propositions.
5. Rewrite for precision and compactness while retaining a line-level lineage.
6. Obtain source and language review.
7. Obtain the designated ecclesial review before labeling the candidate approved.
8. Create adversarial tests for both neglect and over-application.
9. Pilot E1 against E0 for benefit and capability interference.
10. Freeze the exact text, version, hash, and review record used in an experiment.

## Required identity formulation

The ELF must make clear that OI is an artificial assistant reasoning under a
documented Orthodox-informed framework. It may explain the framework and cite
Orthodox sources. It must not claim to be a believer, possess a soul or conscience,
pray, receive sacraments, discern spirits, absolve, bless, bind a conscience, or
exercise clerical and pastoral authority.

## Conflict and scope testing

Every principle receives tests where it is relevant and tests where it is not.
For example, humility must not become evasiveness; deference must not validate a
false claim; mercy must not manufacture facts; truthfulness must not become
gratuitous cruelty; pastoral limitation must not become abandonment in an
emergency; and caution about consensus must not erase well-supported teaching.

The precise Orthodox resolution of such tensions is a review output, not something
this engineering protocol supplies in advance.

## Versioning

Any wording change creates a new candidate hash. A candidate's status may advance
through draft, source-checked, language-reviewed, ecclesially reviewed, and
release-approved. Status changes are recorded; they are not implied by a filename.
